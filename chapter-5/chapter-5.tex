\section{Reading the results}

The empirical findings of this dissertation deliver a sharp, sobering message. Inspect the summary leaderboard in \ref{tab:main-results}, and the initial impression is stark: an unbroken expanse of near-zero numbers, hovering stubbornly between zero and four percent. A naive reaction would dismiss these figures as evidence of a broken benchmark or a failed experiment. That reaction is mistaken. These numbers reflect an unvarnished, mathematically rigorous accounting of what happens when state-of-the-art vision--language--action architectures are adapted and evaluated under realistic consumer hardware constraints against an unforgiving physical simulation.

To interpret this evidence without succumbing to either undue pessimism or unwarranted hype, the data must be separated into three distinct evidentiary domains:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{System and Computational Usability}: Verifies whether the procedurally generated stores, simplified asset geometries, and simulation hooks function reliably at runtime.
  \item \textbf{Demonstration and Adaptation Feasibility}: Confirms whether algorithmic motion planning can successfully supervise multi-modal policies, and whether disparate architectures can ingest a shared observation--action schema.
  \item \textbf{Empirical Policy Competence}: Evaluates the physical performance of the trained checkpoints across our four-tier experimental ladder under the protocol actually executed.
\end{enumerate}

Only the third domain reflects policy capability, and even there, architectural capacity remains inextricably entangled with training step budgets, batch size constraints, parameter-efficient adapters, and low-level control choices. Disentangling these mechanisms is the central objective of this chapter.

\ref{tab:main-results} reports the primary policy evaluation across four models, three experimental scenarios, and seven manipulation tasks. Every task--item--fixture cell pools 100 independent Monte Carlo rollouts. For Pose and Layout, Basket and Board pool three distinct product triplets (300 trials per cell), whereas Floor, Open, and Close pool two triplets (200 trials). In the Pairing condition, each applicable pick-and-place task pools two held-out product triplets (200 trials). A dash (--) designates conditions that are structurally undefined: door tasks operate on fixed structural handles rather than product entities, rendering product-swapping Pairing meaningless. The two composite task columns record zero across all evaluations. A historical $\pi_0$ Pairing cell on floor recovery cannot be reconstructed from archived product records and is preserved as not recoverable (NR) rather than converted into an unsubstantiated integer.

We deliberately refrain from computing a single grand-average score across rows or columns. \section{RQ1: scene generation and computational usability}

Research Question 1 asked whether procedural generation could synthesize structurally coherent, navigable store layouts without sacrificing simulation throughput. On this criterion, the framework succeeds unambiguously. The procedural pipeline generates distinct floor layouts across random seeds rather than repeatedly shifting furniture within a static box. Each layout serializes to a clean JSON descriptor, instantiates in ManiSkill3, and preserves continuous 2.0-meter aisle corridors. Fixtures coalesce into natural, longitudinal multi-unit runs rather than scattering chaotically across the floor (\ref{fig:store-generation}).

However, the mechanism driving this visual order is far more discrete than continuous tensor mathematics might suggest. The tensor field functions as a directional filter along Cartesian sweeps. Because $d = 12$, its effective sensing radius collapses to roughly thirty centimeters, meaning candidate fixtures respond almost exclusively to the single nearest boundary wall. The shelves snap strictly to coordinate axes. The resulting regularity is genuine and functional, but it represents an axis-aligned spatial gate rather than a fluid, multi-angle orientation field.

Shelf population achieves dense visual realism while holding occupancy strictly constant. The designed mechanisms for stock depletion and vertical stacking were excluded from the runtime pipeline; every shelf across all training and evaluation runs is fully stocked. This design choice controls for visual clutter, ensuring that performance variations cannot be blamed on unpredictable gaps in product rows.

The decisive engineering test for RQ1 is plotted in \ref{fig:mesh-performance}. When populating shelves with raw, unoptimized product meshes, simulation stepping times surge from 0.21~seconds for a single unit to 1.24~seconds for four units. At five units, memory exhaustion renders simulation completely impractical. In sharp contrast, our Pareto-optimized simplified meshes maintain stepping durations between 0.23 and 0.42~seconds across the entire scaling curve, effortlessly stepping a massive 118-unit supermarket scene on a single GPU.

Crucially, \ref{fig:mesh-performance} reveals a subtle crossing point: below two units, raw meshes step marginally faster than simplified meshes due to the minor overhead of processing remeshed vertex buffers. But as scene complexity scales, raw meshes choke the rendering pipeline while simplified proxies exhibit near-flat scaling. The practical conclusion is clear: automated mesh decimation is the foundational technological enabler that makes whole-store simulation possible on consumer hardware.

Geometric accessibility holds by construction, as intersecting candidates are culled during sweeping. Visual plausibility was confirmed via direct visual inspection of rendered perspectives. However, navigability was verified only in the sense of geometric clearance: because all policy episodes initialize 1.4~meters in front of the target fixture, store-scale path planning is never exercised by the learned agents.

\section{RQ2: demonstration generation and adaptation}

Research Question 2 evaluated whether an automated motion planner could harvest large-scale demonstrations and whether a single multi-modal interface could support heterogeneous VLA models. 

The demonstration harvester successfully collected 248 verified trajectories across twelve task--item--fixture configurations, amassing 2,976 successful demonstrations and 1,401,169 transitions in approximately ten wall-clock hours on a single RTX 4090. Staged waypoint anchoring coupled with screw interpolation and RRT-Connect fallback delivered a rich training corpus without a single minute of manual teleoperation. A single standardized schema---synchronizing three camera streams, proprioception, and a 13-dimensional action vector---fed four structurally divergent model families without architectural friction. As a data factory, the pipeline fulfilled its mandate.

Yet the adaptation half of RQ2 reveals deep, challenging complexities. The most revealing diagnostic comparison is found in Appendix~\ref{app:training-results}, contrasting the \textbf{Seeds} condition (exact reproduction of training episodes) with the \textbf{Pose} condition (in-domain initial base perturbations). Under Seeds, the robot faces the exact store, shelf, and product placement it was trained on. Under Pose, all visual geometry remains identical, but the robot's initial base position shifts slightly ($\pm 25$~cm translation, $\pm 22.5^{\circ}$ yaw).

Even under this minimal spatial perturbation, performance degrades:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item Octo's board-transfer success rates across three target items drop from 5\%, 3\%, and 3\% under Seeds to 3\%, 1\%, and 1\% under Pose.
  \item $\pi_{0.5}$'s pick-to-basket success rates drop from 2\%, 2\%, and 2\% under Seeds to 1\%, 1\%, and 1\% under Pose.
\end{itemize}
These drops, while numerically small due to the near-zero baseline, demonstrate that learned policies struggle with simple spatial translations. The policies have largely memorized an open-loop motor trajectory tied to a fixed starting coordinate rather than acquiring closed-loop visual feedback control.

SmolVLA exhibits an even more striking behavioral anomaly. Across every pick-and-place task, under every evaluation scenario (including the training Seeds), SmolVLA scores exactly 0\%. Yet on door opening and closing, it achieves success rates between 1\% and 4\%. A model that never once succeeds at grasping an object it observed in 248 demonstrations, but occasionally operates an articulated door, is not experiencing uniform cognitive failure. Door manipulation provides large structural handles and continuous physical guidance during contact, whereas pick-and-place requires sub-centimeter end-effector precision into visual clutter.

Thus, RQ2 yields a divided verdict. On automated demonstration generation, the answer is an unqualified affirmative: 2,976 high-fidelity trajectories were generated and formatted through a unified schema. On policy adaptation, the verdict is deeply constrained. Out of 144 triplet cells in the Seeds/Pose/Layout matrix, 111 cells record at least one successful completion, proving that the models are actively learning. But none achieved dependable autonomy. Because step budgets were capped at 30,000 to 100,000 steps on a single GPU without convergence verification, this study cannot determine whether the demonstration corpus is fundamentally sufficient or whether the models simply suffered from training starvation.

A primary cause lies in the nature of algorithmic demonstrations: the collector retains only successes. The training data presents pristine nominal trajectories, completely omitting recovery actions, missed grasps, or corrective alignment maneuvers. When an adapted policy inevitably drifts from the nominal path, it encounters states never seen in training, triggering rapid, catastrophic failure.

\section{RQ3: atomic-task performance}
\label{sec:atomic}

Under the consumer-hardware adaptation protocol evaluated in this dissertation, no policy achieves dependable autonomous performance on any retail manipulation task. That statement is the central empirical reality of this benchmark.

Across the 144 triplet cells spanning Seeds, Pose, and Layout in Appendix~\ref{app:training-results}, cell success rates range from 0\% to 9\%, with a global mean of 1.5\%. The highest individual cell is Octo closing a showcase door at 9\% under training seeds. Under Pose, no model exceeds 4\% on any task. The policies are not inactive; they move, reach, and interact, but their terminal completion rates hover near the absolute floor.

This outcome must be interpreted through the physical reality of our training protocol (Section~\ref{sec:training}). Each architecture was fine-tuned on a single desktop RTX 4090 GPU at a rigid batch size of 8, operating under preset step limits (30k to 100k steps, or roughly 2.0 to 2.5 wall-clock hours). Three architectures relied on parameter-efficient LoRA updates. No learning rate sweeps, hyperparameter grids, or validation-loss checkpoint selections were performed. The results in \ref{tab:main-results} describe what these four pipelines achieved after a brief fine-tuning session on consumer hardware. They do not define the upper ceiling of these foundation model families when trained to true mathematical convergence on enterprise GPU clusters.

Three common assumptions in the manipulation literature are directly challenged by our empirical data:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Assumption: Articulated doors are trivially easier than shelf picking.} Our data shows no dramatic separation: door success rates (1--4\%) cluster in the exact same band as pick-and-place (0--2\%).
  \item \textbf{Assumption: Retrieving fallen items from the floor is uniquely challenging.} Floor recovery scores sit at the same near-zero baseline as shelf picking (1\% across models under Pose). In fact, under Seeds, Octo's highest task score is floor recovery at 7.5\%.
  \item \textbf{Assumption: Multi-billion-parameter foundation models naturally outperform compact models.} Counter-intuitively, Octo (the smallest model at 93M parameters, and the only one fully fine-tuned without LoRA) achieves the highest individual scores in the benchmark. Whether this advantage stems from full fine-tuning versus LoRA, smaller parameter capacity fitting the tight step budget better, or architectural differences cannot be resolved without a controlled ablation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Distribution shift}
\label{sec:results-shift}

Evaluating transfer across our experimental ladder reveals the severe mathematical limitations of analyzing distribution shift against a near-zero baseline.

Examining the Pose-to-Layout transition in \ref{tab:main-results}, ten of the twenty model--task pairs are numerically identical. Nine shift by less than 0.7 percentage points. A single pair---Octo on door closing---shows a 2.5 percentage point increase (from 8/200 to 13/200). Because baseline performance under Pose hovers between 0\% and 4\%, the observable downward range is almost non-existent. A drop from 1\% to 0\% could represent an environmental failure or stochastic sampling noise across 100 trials. We cannot claim transfer robustness simply because a model scoring 1\% under Pose also scores 1\% under Layout. When an agent is on the floor, it cannot fall any further.

The \textbf{Pairing} condition introduces compositional shift, instructing models to manipulate targets never demonstrated for that task. Here, a clear pattern emerges:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item Octo and SmolVLA collapse to exactly 0\% across all pick-and-place tasks.
  \item $\pi_0$ scores 0\% on Basket and Board, with an unpreserved historical record on Floor (marked NR).
  \item $\pi_{0.5}$ maintains modest non-zero completions: 1\% on Basket, 1\% on Floor, and 3\% on Board.
\end{itemize}
The total collapse of Octo and SmolVLA to zero is consistent with a semantic grounding failure: when confronted with an instruction pairing a familiar skill with an un-demonstrated target, smaller models fail to bind the object reference to the motor primitive.

Crucially, the Layout shift is bounded by the physical initialization: every episode begins 1.4~meters in front of the target fixture, facing it. Translational noise is confined to $\pm 25$~cm, and yaw noise to $\pm 22.5^{\circ}$. The Layout condition tests visual robustness to novel background textures and local fixture geometry; it does not test long-range navigation through a shifting retail store.

\section{Composite tasks}

Both composite tasks---Pick Three Items and Fridge Sequence---record absolute zero across every model, scenario, and trial. Not a single policy succeeded in completing a full multi-stage execution chain.

This finding is striking, but it must not be misinterpreted as a failure of high-level task planning or language decomposition. In our composite protocol, an algorithmic oracle monitors the environment and issues the next atomic instruction only after the preceding subtask is verified. High-level planning was solved by an oracle. The failure belongs entirely to low-level execution and compounding physical error.

The mathematical autopsy of this failure is definitive. Consider a three-stage sequential task where each stage has an independent success probability $p$. The probability of completing the sequence is $P_{\text{composite}} = p^3$. In our atomic evaluations under pose perturbations, observed success probabilities hovered between 1\% and 4\% ($p \in [0.01, 0.04]$). Evaluating the sequence probability:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\text{composite}} = p^3 \le (0.04)^3 = 0.000064 = 0.0064\%.
  \label{eq:composite-math}
\end{equation}
An expected success probability of less than 0.01\% means that in an evaluation budget of 100 trials, observing zero successes is a mathematical certainty. Even before considering error accumulation, base drift, or visual clutter, compounding Bernoulli decay guarantees failure. Multi-stage autonomy cannot emerge until atomic reliability surpasses a critical viability threshold ($p > 0.70$, yielding $p^3 \approx 0.34$).

\ref{tab:failure-share} presents the breakdown across 2,906 annotated failure trajectories. Several macroscopic patterns stand out:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Grasp Acquisition is the Universal Bottleneck}: For Octo, $\pi_0$, and $\pi_{0.5}$, grasping is the largest single failure mode, accounting for 30\%, 30\%, and 40\% of all breakdowns. The robot reaches the target product, aligns its gripper, and closes the fingers, but fails to establish a secure friction hold.
  \item \textbf{SmolVLA Fails Earlier in the Chain}: For SmolVLA, pre-grasp alignment leads at 40\%, followed by target mis-grounding at 20\% and grasp failure at 17\%. SmolVLA frequently fails to position its end-effector within reach of the object, explaining its uniform zero on pick-and-place tasks.
  \item \textbf{Persistent Target Mis-grounding}: Across all four models, target grounding errors remain remarkably consistent, hovering between 18\% and 22\%. Approximately one in every five failures occurs because the policy interacts with the wrong retail package. The models generate kinematically convincing reaches, but direct them toward the wrong brand.
  \item \textbf{Collateral Disturbance in Clutter}: $\pi_{0.5}$ exhibits a 7\% displacement failure rate, noticeably higher than its peers. In densely packed shelves, aggressive arm movements disturb adjacent stock, violating our strict collateral predicates.
\end{itemize}

Before indicting the policy architectures for these grasping failures, our runtime protocol audit uncovered two profound operational constraints that bear directly on physical manipulation.

\textbf{The 50-Step Open-Loop Horizon.} Evaluated policies generate action chunks of 50 steps, which are executed open-loop by the simulator before a new visual observation is rendered. In a high-speed contact environment, 50 simulation steps represent an eternity. If the gripper fingers misalign by a mere centimeter upon contact, the error cannot be corrected until the 50-step chunk expires, by which time the object has slipped, tilted, or collided with neighboring stock. Open-loop chunking blinds the policy during the most critical phase of physical manipulation.

\textbf{The Rigid Spine Constraint ($q_{\text{torso}} = 0$).} In the evaluation client, the torso-lift action dimension is explicitly overwritten with zero at every timestep, permanently pinning the Fetch robot's prismatic spine to its mechanical lowest point. The robot operates with zero vertical torso mobility. This constraint is devastating for tasks like floor recovery: picking an object from the floor and depositing it onto a shelf with an immobilized torso forces the 7-DOF arm to operate at the absolute boundary of its kinematic reach envelope, drastically reducing manipulability and magnifying grasp instability.

The audit reveals that discrepancies divide cleanly into two operational classes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Perceptual and Geometric Relaxations}: Placing pairing items on background shelves, reusing door layouts, initializing the robot 1.4~meters from fixtures, and employing generous basket bounding boxes made visual grounding and spatial reach substantially easier than initially specified.

One item remains unresolved: the Octo evaluation server operated with an observation history length of one, whereas the training configuration in Appendix~\ref{app:training-results} specified two history frames. Because performance does not scale monotonically with history length, Octo's scores carry unresolved provenance.

\section{What the benchmark demonstrates}

Synthesizing our empirical results and protocol audit establishes what this benchmark legitimately proves:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Methodological and System Success}: Automated store generation produces structured, collision-free retail environments; mesh decimation enables large-scale 118-unit simulation on a single GPU; and algorithmic motion planning synthesizes millions of multi-modal transitions without teleoperation.
  \item \textbf{The Consumer Hardware Boundary}: Fine-tuning generalist VLA models on a single 24~GB GPU with batch size 8 and preset step counts yields baseline success rates below 4\%. These scores demonstrate that low-rank adaptation under tight compute budgets does not magically produce robust manipulation controllers.
  \item \textbf{The Primacy of Control Hygiene}: Low manipulation scores are driven primarily by open-loop action chunking and kinematic joint clamping, not high-level linguistic failure. Evaluating foundation models without closed-loop sensory-motor replanning is an evaluation of open-loop trajectory luck.
\end{itemize}

\section{Implications and a targeted ablation programme}
\label{sec:ablations}

Rather than prematurely expanding benchmark scale or adding new models to a leaderboard of near-zero scores, our findings establish a concrete, prioritized diagnostic roadmap:

\textbf{1. Immediate Protocol Repairs (Zero-Retraining Interventions).} Two critical experiments can be executed immediately using existing checkpoints:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Execution Horizon Sweep}: Evaluate unchanged policy checkpoints across action horizons of $H \in \{1, 5, 10, 25, 50\}$. If grasp success surges under tighter feedback loops, the open-loop hypothesis is directly confirmed without retraining a single parameter.
  \item \textbf{Disjoint Door Layouts}: Regenerate door evaluation scenes using truly disjoint floor plan seeds, bringing door evaluation into methodological parity with pick-and-place.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{2. Establishing a Converged In-Domain Baseline.} Select a representative architecture (e.g., Octo or SmolVLA) and train it with validation-loss checkpointing until in-domain Seeds performance stabilizes above 50\%. Only when an agent possesses dependable in-domain competence can downward degradation across Pose, Layout, and Pairing be meaningfully measured.

\textbf{3. Enabling the Full 13-D Kinematics.} Retrain policies with an active torso joint. Unlocking vertical spine movement is essential to provide the kinematic reach necessary for floor recovery and high-shelf picking.

\textbf{4. Enriching Demonstrations with Recovery Trajectories.} Augment the algorithmic demonstration collector by injecting intentional spatial offsets during pre-grasp and transport, recording the planner's corrective maneuvers. Teaching policies how to recover from drift directly attacks the pre-grasp and grasp failure bottleneck.

\textbf{5. Sensory and Planning Oracles.} Deploy diagnostic oracle ablations: provide policies with perfect 3D target bounding boxes to isolate visual grounding from motor control, or pair learned visual grounding with an analytical grasp planner to measure the isolated contribution of physical contact.

This ablation roadmap transforms near-zero results into an actionable blueprint for developing robust, closed-loop mobile manipulation systems for the retail domain.
