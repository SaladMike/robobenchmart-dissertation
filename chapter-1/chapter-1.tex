\section{Retail manipulation as an embodied stress test}

Moving a can of soup between shelf tiers looks straightforward. To an embodied agent, it is a treacherous cascade of coupled physical constraints. The visual prompt names a branded item whose label differs from an adjacent carton by subtle typography; the product sits wedged within millimeters of neighbouring stock; a parallel-jaw gripper must navigate narrow clearances without triggering a retail avalanche; and an unanchored mobile base stopping merely three centimeters off target turns an intended grasp into an outright collision. Articulated fixtures exacerbate the challenge. Swinging a refrigerator door demands approaching from an oblique heading, acquiring a narrow handle, and coordinating continuous mobile chassis repositioning through the arc of the swing. Each obstacle is challenging in isolation. Their collision in a single physical environment turns mobile retail manipulation into an unforgiving stress test for generalist artificial intelligence.

Tabletop benchmarks have driven dramatic progress in robot imitation learning, yet they largely insulate policies from the operational friction of physical stores. Standard computer vision datasets verify whether a bounding-box detector can identify goods on a rack, but passive perception says nothing about whether a physical mobile manipulator can navigate an aisle, align its end-effector, establish stable friction contact, and deposit an item without collateral disturbance. Conversely, isolated tabletop setups risk generating a false sense of security: they measure local grasp kinematics while ignoring the dramatic visual clutter, lighting variations, and geometric shifts encountered across store aisles. A credible retail benchmark must treat the physical layout, the task semantics, and the evaluation protocol as an integrated system.

This dissertation tackles that challenge through an independent reproduction, adaptation, and empirical evaluation of RoboBenchMart, an embodied retail manipulation benchmark developed in ManiSkill3~\cite{taomaniskill3}. ManiSkill3 provides GPU-accelerated rigid-body dynamics, contact simulation, and batch multi-camera rendering. Building on its procedural dark-store plans, textured fixtures, and motion-planning demonstration primitives, this study steps back from speculative architecture proposals to address a foundational empirical question: how do publicly released, generalist vision--language--action (VLA) checkpoints behave when deployed across an independent, multi-tier evaluation ladder of systematic environmental shifts?

Rather than evaluating an idealized simulation under unverified assumptions, we subjected two prominent, structurally divergent VLA architectures---the diffusion transformer Octo and the flow-matching PaliGemma model $\pi_{0.5}$---to a disciplined 42-configuration evaluation protocol. Each configuration was evaluated across 30 Monte Carlo rollouts, yielding 1,260 episodes per model (2,520 episodes in total) supported by full video recordings and runtime logs. The empirical results reveal a profound behavioral divide: while both models score absolute zero on high-clutter pick-and-place tasks, $\pi_{0.5}$ demonstrates measurable, repeatable competence on articulated door manipulation, achieving up to a 25.0\% success rate on door closing. By dissecting this performance split and auditing the executed runtime, this dissertation maps the precise physical fault lines where modern generalist policies fail in unstructured retail aisles.

\section{Problem statement}

The fundamental problem motivating this work is the scarcity of rigorous, auditable empirical benchmarks that evaluate generalist robot policies across progressive distribution shifts in cluttered mobile environments.

While existing manipulation suites supply valuable components---robotics datasets provide pretraining representations, physics engines simulate contact mechanics, and computer vision catalogues offer product models---they rarely evaluate policies under a structured progression moving from familiar training scenes to altered base poses, novel store layouts, and compositional task--item pairings. This leaves two major interpretive blind spots in embodied AI research:

First, claims of generalisation often remain unsubstantiated. A policy might record a non-zero score simply because it memorized an idiosyncratic camera angle or background wall texture, rather than mastering an adaptable motor skill. Without testing identical skills under isolated pose and layout shifts, perceptual overfitting cannot be decoupled from generalizable physical control.

Second, aggregate success rates conceal the underlying causes of physical failure. When a benchmark reports near-zero performance, a scalar score does not explain whether the failure was caused by linguistic mis-grounding, mobile-base steering errors, gripper trajectory drift, or collateral shelf disturbance. A rigorous benchmark must provide the architectural transparency and protocol auditing necessary to separate high-level perception failures from low-level kinematic bottlenecks.

This dissertation addresses these challenges through three central research questions:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.8em,style=nextline]
    \item[\textbf{RQ1}] How can retail layouts, fixtures, and dense product inventories be procedurally instantiated in ManiSkill3 with structural coherence, collision-free aisle clearance, and computational stability on modern GPUs?
    \item[\textbf{RQ2}] How can diverse generalist VLA architectures (diffusion-based Octo vs. flow-matching $\pi_{0.5}$) be interfaced with a standardized multi-view retail observation--action schema, and what operational constraints govern their execution in simulation?
    \item[\textbf{RQ3}] How do released fine-tuned retail VLA checkpoints perform across an independent four-tier distribution shift ladder (`Seeds', `Pose', `Layout', `Pairing'), why does a massive performance gap emerge between articulated fixture interaction and cluttered pick-and-place, and what physical control bottlenecks dictate this outcome?
\end{description}

The answers delivered in this dissertation are empirical, nuanced, and strictly grounded in reproducible data. RQ1 is answered affirmatively through tensor-field orientation gating and multi-objective mesh decimation, enabling stable stepping of multi-shelf supermarkets. RQ2 is answered by demonstrating that heterogeneous policies can be served through standardized inference clients, while uncovering critical operational differences between single-process Octo servers and multi-client shared OpenPI services. RQ3 yields an unvarnished empirical picture: pick-and-place tasks collapse to zero under all conditions, whereas door closing reveals non-zero physical competence (reaching 25.0\% for $\pi_{0.5}$), with protocol auditing demonstrating that open-loop action chunking and rigid torso kinematics dominate the observed failures.

\section{Objectives and scope}

To operationalize these research questions, this project undertook four interconnected technical initiatives. We began by standing up the procedural dark-store simulation in ManiSkill3, resolving runtime package errors, and verifying that procedural store plans assemble without fixture collisions, maintaining mandatory 2.0-meter clear aisle corridors for chassis navigation while populating boards with dense product packaging. 

With the simulation substrate stable, we curated an atomic retail manipulation task portfolio. The suite spans five core task families: picking items to a mobile cart basket, recovering fallen products from the floor, transferring goods between shelf boards, articulating refrigerator doors, and operating showcase merchandisers. Success predicates enforce strict collateral checks: knocking over neighbouring stock invalidates the episode, treating scene integrity as an uncompromising operational requirement.

To serve structurally divergent VLA architectures, we established a unified evaluation client. We adapted the framework to interface with publicly released fine-tuned checkpoints for both Octo (diffusion transformer) and $\pi_{0.5}$ (flow matching), standardizing language conditioning, proprioception, three synchronized RGB camera perspectives (dual shoulder and wrist views), and a 13-dimensional continuous action schema.

Finally, we formulated and executed an independent multi-tier evaluation framework across 42 atomic task configurations under four explicit distribution tiers: \textbf{Seeds} (exact replay of demonstration configurations), \textbf{Pose} (local chassis perturbations), \textbf{Layout} (unseen store geometries and surface textures), and \textbf{Pairing} (zero-shot task--item compositional binding). Evaluating each configuration over 30 rollouts amasses 2,520 fully logged, video-indexed episodes across both models.

Clear empirical boundaries define this study. All experiments were conducted in simulation; physical sim-to-real deployment is not claimed. The evaluated models represent public, upstream fine-tuned checkpoints rather than locally retrained weights. The evaluation focuses exclusively on atomic primitives across 42 configurations; composite multi-stage tasks are documented as benchmark specifications but were not executed in this campaign. Data logging captures terminal outcomes and video recordings, but does not include full continuous HDF5 state-action trajectories. These boundaries ensure that all claims in this dissertation remain strictly verifiable from archived empirical records.

\section{Summary of contributions and empirical insights}

The contributions of this dissertation reside in benchmark reproduction, disciplined empirical evaluation, and rigorous protocol auditing. Rather than proposing speculative model architectures, this work provides a transparent, auditable assessment of generalist robot learning under realistic conditions.

We reproduced, deployed, and verified the RoboBenchMart retail simulation environment in ManiSkill3, resolving runtime import bugs and socket communication bottlenecks to achieve stable batch simulation across procedural store layouts. On top of this verified substrate, we designed and executed an independent 42-configuration evaluation campaign testing released fine-tuned checkpoints of Octo and $\pi_{0.5}$ across 2,520 total episodes. By evaluating each configuration over 30 independent trials, this work establishes an auditable empirical baseline backed by 2,520 archived video recordings, detailed execution logs, and SHA-256 integrity manifests.

The resulting data exposes a dramatic task-dependent performance dichotomy. Across all 1,620 pick-and-place trials, both models score absolute zero (0/1,620). High-clutter grasping in dense retail shelves remains completely unsolved by current released checkpoints. In sharp contrast, $\pi_{0.5}$ achieves substantial, repeatable success on articulated door closing (15/60 under Seeds, 10/60 under Pose, and 12/60 under Layout), confirming that large structural contact surfaces provide passive mechanical guidance that partially forgives end-effector drift.

Crucially, our forensic protocol audit uncovers the exact physical and control bottlenecks limiting policy execution. We demonstrate that executing 50 simulation steps open-loop between visual observations creates a severe sensory blind spot during contact acquisition, while low-level client logic clamping the prismatic torso joint to zero ($q_{\text{torso}} = 0$) cripples vertical workspace reach. Furthermore, we reveal that the door-task Layout condition reuses training store layouts, demonstrating that apparent door transfer reflects texture invariance rather than geometric adaptability.

Together, these findings demonstrate that evaluating embodied foundation models requires rigorous systems hygiene. When a multi-billion-parameter policy fails, the failure does not necessarily reflect an inability to comprehend language or visual scenes; it often stems from open-loop control blindness and kinematic joint immobilization. By establishing an honest, auditable baseline, this dissertation provides an actionable blueprint for developing reliable mobile manipulation systems in physical retail environments.
