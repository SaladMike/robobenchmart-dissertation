\section{Conclusions}

Evaluating generalist foundation models on physical manipulation tasks easily breeds cognitive mirages. It is tempting to observe a multi-billion-parameter Vision--Language--Action model process a visual scene, generate an elaborate internal chain of reasoning, and assume that physical competence will naturally follow. In the physical world, that assumption collides with reality. A policy can correctly identify a brand of tea, comprehend the semantic organization of a retail aisle, and describe the geometry of a parallel gripper, yet still fail to grasp a simple cardboard box because its control loop runs blind for fifty steps or its torso is clamped to the floor.

This dissertation confronted that reality through an independent reproduction, adaptation, and empirical evaluation of the RoboBenchMart benchmark~\cite{soshin2025robobenchmart} within the ManiSkill3 physics engine~\cite{taomaniskill3}. Rather than proposing another speculative foundation architecture or relying on unverified claims of training sufficiency, we established an auditable evaluation pipeline designed backwards from physical interpretability. We deployed released fine-tuned checkpoints for two structurally divergent VLA architectures---the diffusion transformer Octo and the flow-matching PaliGemma model $\pi_{0.5}$---and subjected them to a rigorous 42-configuration evaluation matrix spanning four distribution-shift tiers (``Seeds'', ``Pose'', ``Layout'', and ``Pairing''). With 30 rollouts per configuration, this evaluation amasses 2,520 fully logged, video-indexed episodes.

The three core research questions receive grounded, empirical answers:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{RQ1 (Procedural Usability)}: Answered affirmatively. The procedural pipeline synthesizes topologically varied, collision-free store layouts with guaranteed 2.0-meter aisle clearances. By resolving polygon bottlenecks through automated mesh decimation, the framework effortlessly steps 118-unit fully stocked supermarkets on a single GPU, maintaining simulation step times under 0.42~seconds.
  \item \textbf{RQ2 (Inference Architecture and Protocol Execution)}: Answered affirmatively for inference interfacing, but with vital operational distinctions. A unified multi-view observation schema successfully serves both diffusion and flow-matching backbones. However, runtime auditing revealed critical deployment differences: Octo operated under a hybrid serial/parallel server split, whereas $\pi_{0.5}$ was served via four concurrent clients issuing requests to a shared OpenPI server.
  \item \textbf{RQ3 (Performance and Transfer Under Shift)}: Answered with an unvarnished empirical baseline. Pick-and-place tasks across baskets, shelves, and floors collapse to absolute zero for both models across all conditions (0/1,620 combined). In sharp contrast, articulated door manipulation reveals genuine, non-zero physical competence: $\pi_{0.5}$ achieves a 25.0\% success rate on door closing under nominal Seeds, 16.7\% under Pose perturbations, and 20.0\% under novel Layouts, while Octo completes isolated showcase closures (4/1,260 overall).
\end{enumerate}

The true value of this dissertation lies not in celebrating inflated benchmark leaderboards, but in conducting an honest diagnostic autopsy of physical failure. The performance split between door articulation and cluttered grasping exposes the primacy of passive mechanical constraints: doors succeed because wide surfaces and continuous rotational hinges channel contact force into valid motion, forgiving end-effector drift. Conversely, picking small items from crowded shelves requires millimeter-level closed-loop accuracy. In the benchmark runtime, however, policies are blinded by an open-loop execution chunk of 50 simulator steps, while low-level client logic clamps the robot's prismatic torso to zero ($q_{\text{torso}} = 0$). By unmasking these control bottlenecks and auditing runtime discrepancies, this work provides a transparent foundation for embodied manipulation research.

\section{Limitations}

The findings of this study are bounded by several methodological and physical constraints:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Simulation Boundaries}: All experiments were conducted within ManiSkill3 rigid-body physics. Deformable retail packaging, compliant surface mechanics, sensor calibration noise, dynamic lighting shifts, and human pedestrian traffic were omitted.
  \item \textbf{Evaluated Model Scope}: The evaluation focused exclusively on publicly released fine-tuned checkpoints of Octo and $\pi_{0.5}$. Local fine-tuning from scratch was not conducted, and training dynamics could not be independently ablated.
  \item \textbf{Operational Scope}: Tasks evaluated local approach, shelf picking, floor recovery, and door articulation, but excluded whole-store navigation. In all evaluations, the robot initialized 1.4~meters in front of the target fixture, bounding the evaluation to localized manipulation.
  \item \textbf{Data Container Limits}: While execution logs and 2,520 video recordings are fully archived and verified via SHA-256 manifests, container HDF5 files store 800-byte headers and JSON \texttt{episodes} arrays are empty. Consequently, continuous per-step joint trajectories were not preserved.
  \item \textbf{Door Layout Shift}: As uncovered in our audit, door manipulation tasks in the Layout condition reused training floor plans, varying only textures and starting poses. True geometric transfer was therefore not evaluated for articulated fixtures.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future work}

Rather than prematurely expanding benchmark scale, immediate future work must execute a sequenced programme of diagnostic interventions:

\textbf{Phase 1: Zero-Retraining Horizon Sweeps.}
Using the existing released checkpoints, policies should be evaluated across varying action chunk horizons: $H \in \{1, 5, 10, 25, 50\}$. If grasp success emerges under tighter closed-loop replanning, the open-loop blindness hypothesis will be directly validated without retraining a single parameter.

\textbf{Phase 2: Active Kinematics and Geometric Door Regeneration.}
Enabling the full 13-dimensional action vector by activating the prismatic torso joint ($q_{\text{torso}} > 0$) is essential to provide the vertical reach necessary for floor recovery and high-shelf picking. Simultaneously, door evaluation scenes must be regenerated using genuinely disjoint floor plan seeds to achieve methodological parity with pick-and-place tasks.

\textbf{Phase 3: Recovery Supervision and Local Fine-Tuning.}
Future demonstration collection must incorporate intentional spatial perturbations during pre-grasp and transport, recording corrective trajectories that teach imitation policies how to recover from inevitable drift. Once recovery datasets exist, local fine-tuning studies under controlled compute budgets can systematically isolate the contributions of data scale and architecture.

\section{A staged route to physical validation}

Bridging this simulation benchmark to physical retail deployment requires a disciplined, staged progression:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\Roman*.},leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.3em]
  \item \textbf{Isolated Physical Shelf Cell}: Deploy a mobile manipulator before a single, standardized retail shelving unit matching simulator dimensions. Equipping the platform with calibrated RGB-D cameras and parallel-jaw grippers will isolate the true sim-to-real gap: determining whether physical failures stem from visual domain shift, tactile friction discrepancies, or controller latency.
  \item \textbf{Clutter and Packaging Compliance}: Introduce real-world commercial packaging---glossy cardboard, deformable potato chip bags, transparent plastic bottles---evaluating how material compliance and non-rigid physics degrade simulated grasp envelopes.
  \item \textbf{Whole-Store Mobility and Safety Envelopes}: Integrate local manipulation policies with global SLAM navigation across physical store aisles. Deploy mandatory real-time safety envelopes: dynamic velocity clamping, collision retreat behaviors, and human exclusion zones.
  \item \textbf{Throughput and Intervention Auditing}: Shift performance reporting from binary success rates to commercial throughput metrics: items picked per hour, non-target damage rates, and human intervention frequencies.
\end{enumerate}

The central lesson of this dissertation is pragmatic: evaluating artificial intelligence on physical robots demands rigorous systems hygiene. When a generalist foundation model fails to pick an item from a shelf, the failure is rarely a high-level cognitive deficiency. It is far more often the predictable consequence of an open-loop controller executing blindly into clutter or a mechanical spine pinned to zero. By benchmarking released checkpoints across 2,520 rollouts, contrasting door mechanics against cluttered grasping, and executing a transparent protocol audit, this dissertation replaces speculation with auditable evidence, offering a concrete foundation for building autonomous mobile manipulation systems that genuinely work in the real world.
