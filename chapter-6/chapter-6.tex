\section{Conclusions}

This dissertation set out to implement a retail mobile-manipulation benchmark and use it to study four generalist policies. The implementation produced usable scenes, demonstrations and a common policy interface. The policy comparison did not reach the same standard of evidence. All adaptation ran on one NVIDIA RTX 4090, at batch size eight and preset step budgets, and no checkpoint was selected by validation-based convergence.

The main table must be read within that boundary. No policy exceeds 4\% on any task under Pose, while the detailed Seeds/Pose/Layout cells range from zero to nine successes in one hundred trials. Three models use low-rank adaptation and Octo is fully fine-tuned. The rows therefore describe four fixed-step pipelines, not architectures trained to a common endpoint.

RQ1 is answered for procedural variation, collision constraints and geometric passage provision. The recorded original-mesh timing series ends at four shelving units; the simplified series includes a 118-unit stocked scene. Visual plausibility was inspected rather than measured, and no episode required store-scale navigation. The engineering half of RQ2 is stronger: the collector produced 2,976 successful trajectories and 1,401,169 transitions in about ten hours, and one observation--action schema served all four model families.

The adaptation half of RQ2 remains unresolved. Although 111 of 144 Seeds/Pose/Layout triplet cells contain at least one success, none of the four pipelines became dependable. Compute, corpus design, optimisation and task difficulty remain entangled. RQ3 is unresolved for a related reason. Ten of twenty Pose--Layout point differences are zero, nine are no larger than two thirds of a percentage point, and one is a 2.5-point increase; a 0--4\% baseline leaves little room to observe a fall, while the retained aggregates cannot support design-aware contrasts. Door-task Layout changes no scene geometry at all.

The failure shares offer exploratory detail, not a causal answer. SmolVLA is labelled most often at pre-grasp, whereas grasp is the largest category for the two $\pi$ pipelines; the underlying episode identifiers, however, were not retained for re-aggregation. The protocol audit adds firmer facts. A separate code reading prompted the audit, and the author reproduced each confirmed discrepancy: nominally unseen products appeared on training shelves, four door tasks reused training scenes, and the robot always began near the correct fixture. At the same time, fifty actions ran between observations and the torso never moved. These conditions narrow the study; their net effect cannot be calculated after the fact.

The contribution is consequently an implemented dissertation project whose generation, simulation, collection and interface components were exercised, together with a candid record of the study run on them. It is not a new architecture, metric or policy ranking. Answering the transfer question requires convergence-checked adaptation, matched evaluation conditions and complete episode-level records.

\section{Limitations}

The study is entirely simulated on one principal robot embodiment. It omits deformable packaging, transparent materials, sensor and calibration error, people, moving equipment and explicit safety behaviour. The task suite covers picking and articulation, but not scanning, inventory verification or store-scale travel.

Its statistical record is thinner than the episode count suggests. Episode pairing and layout grouping were not retained, so the pooled task rates cannot support design-aware contrasts. Failure labels come from one annotator, with no agreement statistic, and the earliest-primary rule hides downstream effects. Composite tasks are oracle-decomposed; their zero scores concern full-sequence execution rather than autonomous planning, while missing progress records prevent a finer account.

Training sufficiency remains the binding limitation. Preset step counts, unmatched adaptation methods and the absence of validation-based checkpoint selection make causal attribution impossible. Section~\ref{sec:verification} separately records where the executed protocol departed from the written design.

\section{Future work}

Before anything is extended, one experiment should be run, and it is not a new one. Adapt a single policy with validation-based checkpoint selection until its in-domain success no longer improves, then re-evaluate it on the scenes and conditions already built. The resulting learning curve and held-out success rate would show whether the preset-step run stopped before measurable performance emerged. If performance remained near zero after that check, data, optimisation and task difficulty would still need controlled ablations before any one of them could be named as the limiting factor. Everything else in this section depends on establishing that training baseline first.

A corrected rerun should be folded into the same pass. It would enable the torso and head commands already present in the nominal thirteen-dimensional interface, generate genuinely disjoint evaluation scenes for the four door tasks, replace the duplicated configuration seeds, extend basket collateral checking to the whole scene and sweep the execution horizon rather than fixing it at fifty. Enabling those joints changes the effective control distribution even though it does not change the nominal action dimension. Because every reported demonstration and evaluation fixed torso lift and head pan at zero, the corrected comparison requires demonstrations and checkpoints trained under the enabled-control regime.

The most immediate extension after that is an automatic diagnostic evaluator. Ground-truth simulator state could provide target-selection accuracy, local approach error, pre-grasp pose error, grasp retention time, non-target displacement and conditional subtask success. Episode-level outputs would support confidence intervals and make failure analysis less dependent on manual labels.

Data collection should add recovery. Perturbing demonstrations at approach and grasp stages, then planning or teleoperating corrections, would teach the policy what to do after small deviations. A mixed corpus could retain efficient planned successes while adding human demonstrations where contact is difficult. Retail-specific pretraining is another promising hypothesis, but it should be tested through controlled data-scale and domain-mixture ablations rather than assumed from model ranking.

Scene generation can also move closer to measured reality. Store-plan statistics, calibrated camera noise, friction variation, compliant packaging and dynamic obstacles would improve external validity. Dynamic level of detail based on camera visibility could replace the current near-versus-background simplification rule. Finally, physical validation should begin with matched atomic tasks and identical success predicates, then quantify the sim-to-real gap before attempting a full autonomous order.

\section{A staged route to physical validation}

Physical validation should begin with one matched shelf cell, calibrated sensors and the same atomic predicates used in simulation. Trials paired by item and initial pose would reveal whether the first gap appears in perception, contact or controller mapping. Packaging gloss, shelf height, mass, friction and exposure could then be varied one at a time instead of folded into a single real-world score.

A full aisle and composite orders belong later. They require limited base speed, collision monitoring, an emergency stop, exclusion zones and conservative recovery when object state becomes uncertain. Throughput should be reported together with interventions and recovery burden; otherwise frequent human assistance disappears behind an apparently autonomous score.

Physical work also reintroduces people and data. Camera retention, access control and model logs need to be specified before collection, while worker feedback can expose blocked routes, awkward resets or unpredictable motion that simulation misses. These are evaluation variables, not administrative afterthoughts.

The result to carry forward is narrow but useful: the four fixed-step adapted pipelines remained fragile under the executed protocol. This dissertation project makes that outcome inspectable and identifies the controlled experiments needed to understand it, without presenting the result as a verdict on the underlying policy families.
