\section{Conclusions}

Evaluating generalist foundation models on physical manipulation tasks is prone to cognitive mirages. It is easy to watch a multi-billion-parameter Vision--Language--Action model process a visual scene, generate an elaborate internal chain of reasoning, and assume that physical competence will naturally follow. In the physical world, that assumption collides with reality. A policy can correctly identify a brand of tea, understand the semantic structure of a retail store, and describe the physics of a parallel gripper, yet still fail to grasp a simple cardboard box because its control loop runs blind for fifty steps or its torso is clamped to the floor.

This dissertation confronted that reality by reproducing, adapting, and stress-testing the RoboBenchMart benchmark~\cite{soshin2025robobenchmart} within the ManiSkill3 physics engine~\cite{taomaniskill3}. Operating as an independent researcher under realistic consumer computational constraints---a single desktop NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU equipped with 24~GB of VRAM---we built an end-to-end evaluation pipeline designed backwards from the demands of physical interpretability. We successfully operationalized the procedural store generator, configured an analytical mesh decimation pipeline to enable scalable batch simulation, deployed the algorithmic demonstration harvester to generate 2,976 verified trajectories without human teleoperation, and constructed an independent multi-tier evaluation suite serving four structurally divergent VLA architectures.

The empirical findings of this project demand unflinching intellectual honesty. Across four adapted model families (Octo, SmolVLA, $\pi_0$, and $\pi_{0.5}$) evaluated over 100-trial Monte Carlo rollouts across an experimental ladder of environmental shifts, task-level success rates clustered persistently between zero and four percent. Not a single policy achieved dependable autonomy on any atomic task, and every policy scored absolute zero on multi-stage composite sequences. 

The three core research questions receive clear, rigorous answers:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{RQ1 (Procedural Usability)}: Answered affirmatively. The procedural pipeline synthesizes topologically varied, collision-free store layouts with guaranteed 2.0-meter aisle clearances. By resolving polygon bottlenecks through automated mesh decimation, the framework effortlessly steps 118-unit fully stocked supermarkets on a single GPU, maintaining simulation step times under 0.42~seconds.
  \item \textbf{RQ2 (Demonstration and Adaptation Feasibility)}: Yields a divided verdict. As a demonstration factory, the algorithmic motion planner is a proven success: harvesting 2,976 collision-free trajectories and 1.4 million transitions in ten hours, seamlessly feeding heterogeneous models through a unified schema. But on policy adaptation, consumer-hardware fine-tuning failed to produce robust controllers. While 111 out of 144 triplet cells in our in-domain matrix achieved non-zero completions, baseline reliability remained near the floor.
  \item \textbf{RQ3 (Performance and Transfer Under Shift)}: Answered with rigorous negative evidence. Under preset step budgets on a single GPU, generalist VLAs do not exhibit dependable zero-shot or few-shot transfer across retail geometric and compositional shifts. Furthermore, mathematical analysis proves that zero success on composite tasks ($p^3 \le 0.0064\%$) is an inescapable statistical certainty stemming from low atomic success probabilities ($p \approx 0.01\sim 0.04$), rather than an independent breakdown of sequential task reasoning.
\end{enumerate}

The true value of this dissertation lies not in celebrating inflated benchmark leaderboards, but in conducting a forensic autopsy of physical failure. Authoring an 11-stage chronological coding taxonomy across 2,906 unsuccessful rollouts revealed that breakdowns concentrate overwhelmingly at pre-grasp alignment and grasp contact, accompanied by persistent 18--22\% target mis-grounding. Our systematic protocol audit unmasked the underlying physical culprits: an open-loop execution horizon of fifty steps that blinded policies during dynamic contact, a prismatic torso joint permanently clamped to zero that crippled vertical reach, and demonstration datasets devoid of recovery behaviors. The primary contribution of this work is an inspectable, reproducible testbed and a candid diagnostic blueprint for embodied robot learning.

\section{Limitations}

The conclusions of this study are bounded by several methodological and physical constraints:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Simulation Boundaries}: All experiments were conducted within ManiSkill3 rigid-body physics. Deformable retail packaging, compliant surfaces, tactile friction, sensor calibration noise, dynamic lighting shifts, and human pedestrian traffic were omitted.
  \item \textbf{Operational Scope}: Tasks evaluated local approach, shelf retrieval, floor picking, and door articulation, but excluded whole-store global navigation, barcode scanning, RFID tracking, and inventory auditing. In all evaluations, the robot initialized 1.4~meters in front of the target fixture, bounding the evaluation to localized manipulation.
  \item \textbf{Statistical Preservation}: Empirical records reflect pooled cell summaries. Raw step-by-step rollout logs and cross-scenario episode pairings were not fully archived, precluding design-aware mixed-effects modeling and paired bootstrap confidence intervals.
  \item \textbf{Single-Annotator Taxonomy}: Failure categories were assigned by a single researcher under an earliest-primary criterion. While highly effective at surfacing macroscopic physical bottlenecks, the absence of inter-rater reliability metrics means failure shares must be interpreted descriptively rather than as exact point measurements.
  \item \textbf{Hardware and Convergence Constraints}: Model adaptation was constrained to a single 24~GB GPU with batch size 8, preset step counters (30k--100k steps), and parameter-efficient LoRA adapters without validation-loss checkpoint selection. The results document the capabilities of quickly adapted pipelines under consumer hardware constraints, not the theoretical asymptotic limits of converged models.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future work}

Rather than prematurely expanding benchmark scale, immediate future work must execute a sequenced, prioritized programme of diagnostic interventions:

\textbf{Phase 1: Zero-Retraining Protocol Repairs.}
Using the existing trained checkpoints, two critical hypotheses can be tested immediately:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Horizon Sweeping}: Re-evaluating existing checkpoints under tighter action chunk horizons ($H \in \{1, 5, 10, 25, 50\}$). If grasp success increases under closed-loop replanning, the open-loop blindness hypothesis is confirmed without spending a single hour on retraining.
  \item \textbf{Disjoint Door Evaluation}: Re-generating door evaluation scenes using truly disjoint floor plan seeds, establishing methodological parity between articulated fixture tasks and pick-and-place families.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Phase 2: Active Kinematics and Converged Baselines.}
Retrain a representative baseline (such as Octo or SmolVLA) with full activation of the 13-dimensional action schema, unpinning the prismatic torso joint ($q_{\text{torso}} > 0$). Incorporating validation-loss checkpoint selection until in-domain Seeds success stabilizes above 50\% is essential to establish a reliable baseline against which environmental transfer losses can be meaningfully measured.

\textbf{Phase 3: Automated Metric Logging and Recovery Supervision.}
Replace manual failure annotation with automated simulator-state evaluators logging continuous physical metrics: 3D bounding-box IoU, gripper contact forces, and non-target displacement distances. Simultaneously, enrich the demonstration collector by injecting deliberate spatial perturbations during trajectory generation, teaching imitation policies the essential art of recovering from small motor errors.

\section{A staged route to physical validation}

Bridging this simulation benchmark to physical retail deployment requires a disciplined, staged progression:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\Roman*.},leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.3em]
  \item \textbf{Isolated Physical Shelf Cell}: Deploy a mobile manipulator before a single, standardized retail shelving unit matching simulator dimensions. Equipping the platform with calibrated RGB-D cameras and parallel-jaw grippers will isolate the true sim-to-real gap: determining whether physical failures stem from visual domain shift, tactile friction discrepancies, or controller latency.
  \item \textbf{Clutter and Packaging Compliance}: Introduce real-world commercial packaging---glossy cardboard, deformable potato chip bags, transparent plastic bottles---evaluating how material compliance and non-rigid physics degrade simulated grasp envelopes.
  \item \textbf{Whole-Store Mobility and Safety Envelopes}: Integrate local manipulation policies with global SLAM navigation across physical store aisles. Deploy mandatory real-time safety envelopes: dynamic velocity clamping, collision retreat behaviors, and human exclusion zones.
  \item \textbf{Throughput and Intervention Auditing}: Shift performance reporting from binary success rates to commercial throughput metrics: items picked per hour, non-target damage rates, and human intervention frequencies.
\end{enumerate}

The ultimate takeaway of this dissertation is pragmatic: evaluating artificial intelligence on physical robots demands rigorous systems hygiene. When a multi-billion-parameter foundation model fails to pick an item from a shelf, the failure is rarely a high-level cognitive deficiency. It is far more often the predictable consequence of an open-loop controller executing blindly into clutter or a mechanical spine pinned to zero. By reproducing RoboBenchMart under realistic consumer hardware constraints, providing an unflinching autopsy of 2,906 failure rollouts, and executing a transparent protocol audit, this dissertation strips away the hype surrounding generalist robot learning. It replaces speculation with auditable evidence, offering a concrete, disciplined foundation for building autonomous mobile manipulation systems that genuinely work in the real world.
