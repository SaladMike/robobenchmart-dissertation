\toprule Task & Objective & Principal success conditions\\\midrule\endhead
Pick to basket & Put a named shelf product in the cart. & A matching target is inside the basket; non-target stock is not displaced; the robot is static.\\
Pick from floor & Return a floor product to its shelf region. & The product is near the correct group; other items remain in place; the robot is static.\\
Board to board & Move a named product between shelf levels. & The target reaches the destination board; neighbours are not moved; the robot is static.\\
Open fridge & Open the designated refrigerated door. & The articulation passes the open threshold and the robot is static.\\
Close fridge & Close an initially open refrigerated door. & The articulation passes the closed threshold and the robot is static.\\
\bottomrule\end{longtable}

The code also contains vertical-showcase door variants. These share the articulation skill family but address one of four named doors. Their presence explains why software-level task counts can exceed the five atomic columns in the main result table. Composite tasks either repeat pick-to-basket for three products or execute open, pick and close. An oracle supplies the atomic sequence; the headline metric gives no partial credit.
