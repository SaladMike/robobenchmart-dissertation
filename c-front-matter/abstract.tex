Most robot manipulation benchmarks live comfortably on pristine tabletops or in spacious domestic kitchens. Retail dark stores are far less accommodating. Shelves are densely packed, branded packages differ by subtle typography, and physical acquisition demands sub-centimetre precision from a mobile base.

This dissertation explores that unforgiving setting by reproducing, adapting, and empirically expanding the RoboBenchMart benchmark within ManiSkill3. Building on its procedural store generator, fixture layouts, and motion-planned demonstrations, we constructed an independent evaluation framework to examine how generalist vision-language-action (VLA) policies respond to systematic distribution shifts. Four distinct model families---Octo, SmolVLA, $\pi_0$, and $\pi_{0.5}$---were fine-tuned and tested under an uncompromising consumer hardware constraint: a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, batch size eight, fixed step budgets, and parameter-efficient low-rank adaptation.

The empirical outcome is sobering. Under in-domain pose perturbations, task success rates peaked at 4\%, with triplet-level evaluations clustering between zero and four completions per hundred trials. Composite multi-stage tasks collapsed to zero. Rather than obscuring these numbers behind coarse summary metrics or dismissing them as proof that foundation architectures cannot manipulate goods, this study treats the near-zero baseline as a diagnostic starting point. Through an eleven-stage taxonomy spanning 2,906 failure rollouts and a subsequent audit of the executed runtime, we traced where the manipulation chain actually unravels. The primary obstacles were not merely semantic misunderstandings. They were structural and mechanical: an open-loop execution chunk of fifty simulator steps that blinded the robot during critical contact, a prismatic torso joint pinned at zero that crippled vertical reach, and demonstration datasets devoid of recovery trajectories.

The result is not a polished leaderboard, but an honest empirical post-mortem from a single-card research environment. We delineate what consumer compute can and cannot resolve, demonstrate why baseline training sufficiency must precede transfer claims, and detail the precise control-rate, kinematic, and data-collection revisions required before generalist policies can reliably operate in retail aisles.
