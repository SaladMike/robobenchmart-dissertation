Generalist robot manipulation policies thrive on clean tabletops. Modern grocery dark stores, by contrast, offer no such hospitality. Shelves are jammed with visually ambiguous consumer packaged goods; packages shift under the slightest contact; and reaching into tight fixtures demands millimeter-level precision from an unanchored mobile base.

This dissertation presents an independent empirical evaluation and protocol audit of the RoboBenchMart benchmark in ManiSkill3. Rather than relying on unverified claims of training sufficiency or speculative multi-model leaderboards, we evaluate two prominent, structurally divergent vision--language--action (VLA) architectures using their publicly released fine-tuned checkpoints: the diffusion-transformer policy Octo and the flow-matching PaliGemma-based model $\pi_{0.5}$. We systematically test both models across a rigorous 42-configuration evaluation matrix spanning four distribution-shift tiers: nominal Seeds, local base Pose perturbations, unseen store Layouts, and compositional task--item Pairings. Each configuration is executed for 30 rollouts, yielding 1,260 evaluated episodes per model (2,520 episodes total), backed by indexed video recordings and run logs.

The empirical results reveal a profound behavioral divide. Across all 1,620 evaluated pick-and-place episodes spanning carts, shelves, and floors, both models score absolute zero. Neither Octo nor $\pi_{0.5}$ successfully completed a single item transfer. Yet when tasked with manipulating articulated refrigerator and showcase doors, distinct physical competencies emerge. Octo achieves isolated completions on showcase doors (4/1,260 total), while $\pi_{0.5}$ delivers repeatable door-closing behavior, registering a 25.0\% success rate under nominal Seeds, 16.7\% under Pose perturbations, and 20.0\% under novel Layouts (39/1,260 total).

This stark performance split provides a crucial diagnostic window into embodied foundation models. Door articulation succeeds because large rigid surfaces and continuous physical contact provide passive mechanical guidance that forgives slight end-effector drift. Conversely, cluttered grasping demands tight closed-loop sensory-motor correction. In the benchmark runtime, however, policies are forced to execute 50 simulator steps open-loop between visual observations, blinding them during contact acquisition, while low-level client logic clamps the prismatic torso joint permanently to zero. By identifying these control bottlenecks, verifying the provenance of released checkpoints, and establishing a fully auditable 2,520-rollout baseline, this work transforms near-zero manipulation scores into actionable systems insights for real-world mobile retail robotics.
