Progress in generalist robot learning has been measured largely in tabletop and household settings. Those environments are useful, yet they leave open a practical question: whether a policy adapted to familiar manipulation problems remains dependable when geometry, object density, visual appearance, and work procedures change together. Retail dark stores provide a demanding test. Products are numerous and visually similar, shelves occupy several heights and depths, aisles restrict base motion, and a useful robot must interact with both loose items and articulated fixtures without disturbing nearby inventory.

This dissertation presents a simulation and evaluation suite that combines procedural store-plan generation, configurable product arrangement, motion-planning-based trajectory sampling, and atomic and composite mobile-manipulation tasks. Tensor-field-guided, collision-aware fixture placement produces organised aisle structure. Product placement supports controlled variation in pose and surrounding texture; shelf depletion was designed, but it was neither implemented nor exercised in the reported study. Asset standardisation and mesh simplification make densely stocked scenes computationally tractable. Separating serialised scene specifications from ManiSkill task environments allows layout, product arrangement, target item, texture, and robot initial pose to be varied and reconstructed. These capabilities are broader than the reported policy experiment, whose headline comparison excludes unseen shelf arrangements and completely unseen products.

The benchmark uses 2,976 successful demonstrations to adapt four generalist vision-language-action policy families---Octo, SmolVLA, $\pi_0$, and $\pi_{0.5}$---on five atomic task families: picking an item to a basket, recovering an item from the floor, transferring an item between shelf boards, and opening or closing a refrigerated fixture. Evaluation compares initial-pose variation, unseen layouts and textures, and unseen task--item pairings in which the products were observed in other training tasks.

All adaptation was carried out on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090, which bounds what the policy comparison can establish and is therefore stated before its results. That budget permitted a batch size of eight, step counts between thirty and a hundred thousand, and low-rank adaptation rather than full fine-tuning for three of the four models; each run stopped when its preset counter expired, and none was assessed with a validation-based convergence criterion. Under this setup, no policy exceeds four percent success on any task in the mildest evaluation condition, and the per-item table spans zero to nine successes in a hundred trials. The task-level values pool two or three equal-sized triplets and are reported as rounded percentages, but all calculations use the unrounded pooled counts. From those counts, ten of the twenty Pose--Layout differences are zero, nine have magnitudes no greater than two thirds of a percentage point, and one is a 2.5-point increase. These differences can be calculated, but the 0--4\% Pose baseline sharply limits the observable downward range, and a design-aware interval for the condition contrast cannot be reconstructed from the retained aggregates. The data therefore cannot distinguish a small degradation from no change or improvement. Whether current generalist policies transfer across retail-specific shift remains open in both directions. Two observations survive that limitation. The aggregate failure distribution shows descriptive differences across the four constrained runs, with SmolVLA stopping before contact while the $\pi$ models reach the object and fail at grasp; and two properties of the evaluation setup---a fifty-step open-loop execution horizon and a torso joint fixed throughout every episode---bear directly on that bottleneck regardless of training budget. Manual analysis of a reported sample of 2,906 unsuccessful episodes identifies target grounding, pre-grasp positioning, and grasping as the principal recurring stages of breakdown.

Partway through the study the experimental implementation was examined independently against the documented protocol, and every discrepancy retained in the review was reproduced before being accepted. Several evaluation conditions turned out to be more permissive than described: the products designated unseen stand on the shelves of the training scenes, the unseen-scene condition for the door tasks reuses the training scenes, the robot begins every episode close to and facing the correct fixture, and, for basket picking, the accepted goal region and collateral-damage check are more permissive than originally intended. Two conditions ran harsher: a fifty-step open-loop execution horizon and a torso joint held fixed throughout. The permissive discrepancies show that the intended distribution shifts alone did not cause the near-zero rates. The net effect of correcting the full protocol, however, cannot be predicted without a rerun, and two mechanistic explanations are therefore retained only as hypotheses with experiments attached.

The conclusion divides between the instrument and the study run on it. The generator produces structured, collision-free plans with geometric passage space, and mesh simplification moves the practical ceiling from four shelving units to a hundred and eighteen. Visual plausibility was inspected rather than measured, however, and store-scale navigation was not exercised by the reported episodes. One observation--action schema nevertheless fed four architectures with almost nothing in common. The policy study does not carry the comparison it was designed to make because training sufficiency was not established before evaluation, the familiar-condition rates sit close to the floor, and the retained aggregates do not support design-aware condition contrasts. More permissive tests still returned near-zero rates, but fixed-step runs without convergence checks cannot identify whether the limiting factor was compute, the corpus, the optimisation configuration or the tasks. By linking environment generation, demonstration collection, evaluation, failure analysis, and a documented account of the gap between design and implementation, this work provides a reproducible platform for future tests of retail-specific generalisation and specifies the controlled adaptation experiment required before that question can be answered. Its evidence remains limited to simulation, one principal robot embodiment, a bounded task set that excludes store-scale navigation, and a single-GPU training budget; these constraints define the priorities for future validation.
