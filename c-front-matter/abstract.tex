Progress in generalist robot learning has been measured largely in tabletop and household settings. Those environments are useful, yet they leave open a practical question: whether a policy adapted to familiar manipulation problems remains dependable when geometry, object density, visual appearance, and work procedures change together. Retail dark stores provide a demanding test. Products are numerous and visually similar, shelves occupy several heights and depths, aisles restrict base motion, and a useful robot must interact with both loose items and articulated fixtures without disturbing nearby inventory.

This dissertation presents a simulation and evaluation suite that combines procedural store-plan generation, configurable product arrangement, motion-planning-based trajectory sampling, and atomic and composite mobile-manipulation tasks. Tensor-field-guided, collision-aware fixture placement produces organised aisle structure. Product placement supports controlled variation in pose, shelf occupancy and texture, while asset standardisation and mesh simplification make densely stocked scenes computationally tractable. Separating serialised scene specifications from ManiSkill task environments allows layout, product arrangement, target item, texture, and robot initial pose to be varied and reconstructed. These capabilities are broader than the reported policy experiment, whose headline comparison excludes unseen shelf arrangements and completely unseen products.

The benchmark uses 2,976 successful demonstrations to adapt four generalist vision-language-action policy families---Octo, SmolVLA, $\pi_0$, and $\pi_{0.5}$---on five atomic task families: picking an item to a basket, recovering an item from the floor, transferring an item between shelf boards, and opening or closing a refrigerated fixture. Evaluation compares initial-pose variation, unseen layouts and textures, and unseen task--item pairings in which the products were observed in other training tasks.

All adaptation was carried out on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090, which bounds what the policy comparison can establish and is therefore stated before its results. That budget permitted step counts one to two orders of magnitude below the four policies' published recipes, a batch size of eight, and low-rank adaptation rather than full fine-tuning for three of the four models. Under it, no policy exceeds four percent success on any task in the mildest evaluation condition, the per-item table spans zero to nine successes in a hundred trials, and the confidence intervals of every pair of cells overlap. The distribution-shift comparison consequently cannot be made: measuring degradation requires in-domain performance to degrade, and across twenty model--task pairs the change from familiar to unseen scenes is zero percentage points in fifteen of them. Whether current generalist policies transfer across retail-specific shift remains open in both directions. Two observations survive the low rates because they do not depend on them: the failure distribution differs systematically between models, with SmolVLA stopping before contact while the $\pi$ models reach the object and fail at grasp; and two properties of the evaluation setup---a fifty-step open-loop execution horizon and a torso joint fixed throughout every episode---bear directly on that bottleneck regardless of training budget. Manual analysis of 2,906 unsuccessful episodes identifies target grounding, pre-grasp positioning, and grasping as the principal recurring stages of breakdown.

Partway through the study the experimental implementation was examined independently against the protocol as documented, and every discrepancy raised was reproduced before being accepted. Several evaluation conditions turned out to be more permissive than described: the products designated unseen stand on the shelves of the training scenes, the unseen-scene condition for the door tasks reuses the training scenes, the robot begins every episode close to and facing the correct fixture, and the collateral-damage check is narrower than the equation defining it. A smaller number of conditions ran harsher, chiefly a fifty-step open-loop execution horizon and a torso joint held fixed throughout. The asymmetry matters for interpretation. Because nearly every discrepancy loosened the test, the negative result survives it, while two of the mechanistic explanations offered for that result do not and are withdrawn to hypotheses with experiments attached.

The conclusion divides between the instrument and the study run on it. The instrument works: store plans generate with structured variation and stay navigable, mesh simplification moves the practical ceiling from four shelving units to a hundred and eighteen, and one observation--action schema fed four architectures with almost nothing in common. The policy study does not carry the comparison it was designed to make, because the available compute could not adapt the policies far enough for the evaluation conditions to discriminate between them. A test made more permissive than described, returning near-zero rates under a budget far below the recipes being tested, is a test limited by that budget rather than evidence that the tasks are infeasible. By linking environment generation, demonstration collection, evaluation, failure analysis, and a documented account of the gap between design and implementation, this work provides a reproducible platform for diagnosing retail-specific generalisation, and specifies the single adaptation experiment that would allow it to answer the question it was built for. Its evidence remains limited to simulation, one principal robot embodiment, a bounded task set that excludes store-scale navigation, and a single-GPU training budget; these constraints define the priorities for future validation.
